What Exactly Is a Sunset Clause?

Plain English: “If the apartment isn’t finished by 30 June 2026, either of us can cancel the contract.”

Legal name: Clause 9.3 in 90 % of REIV off-the-plan contracts.

Real-world effect: Developer can walk away, sell your unit for $180k more, and keep your 10 % deposit.

Trap #1 – The “Two Free Extensions” Trick

“The Vendor may extend the Sunset Date twice by written notice.”

→ Developer emails you on 29 June 2026: “Extended to 2027.” Then again to 2028. You have zero say.

Real case: $180,000 profit to developer, $180,000 deposit gone.

Trap #2 – The “Keep Your Deposit” Bomb

If YOU try to cancel after the sunset date, the contract says: “Purchaser forfeits the deposit.”

→ You’re trapped paying rent for 4 years while the developer decides.

Trap #3 – No Definition of “Finished”

Clause says “registered plan” but never defines “registration”.

→ Developer claims “COVID delays” = not their fault = sunset triggered.

The 7-Word Safe Clause We Paste Into Every Contract

“Any extension of the Sunset Date requires the Purchaser’s prior written consent.”

That’s it. 7 words. Copy → paste → initial → sleep easy.
